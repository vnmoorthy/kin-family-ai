## Likely judge questions (keep each answer under 20 seconds)

**1. Why won't families churn?**
"The memory compounds. After a month Kin knows who passes which store, every allergy, every school rhythm, and leaving means wiping your family's brain. It's also multiplayer: to churn, the whole family has to leave, not one person. And the daily brief plus the streak make it a habit, not a tool you remember once a month."

**2. Isn't this Cozi, Skylight, or Maple?**
"Those are great places to *put* family information, but someone, usually Mom, still has to enter it, decide who does it, and chase them. Kin is the coordinator, not the container. You tell it anything, even a photo, and it decides who should handle it from memory and nudges that person's phone. Skylight is a screen on the wall; Kin is in everyone's pocket, with private memory per person."

**3. What about privacy between family members?**
"There are two hard-separated memory namespaces: the family memory, visible only to members who joined with your family's code, and a 'Just me' memory per person. Private memories are only used to answer *that* person. Anything sent to someone else is generated from family memory only. That's why the surprise party stays a surprise."

**4. Why now?**
"Three things just got good enough at the same time. Vision models can reliably read a fridge, a flyer or a worksheet. Memory layers like Supermemory make long-term, per-namespace recall easy. And reactive backends like Convex make real-time multiplayer trivial; we built this in a day. Families already talk to AI, one person at a time. We're making it multiplayer."

**5. How do you get the whole family on it?**
"The first nudge is the invite. Mom gets value as soon as one other person joins, and every family invites three to five members by code. Grandparents and nannies don't even need the app; they'll get nudges over SMS. Kids come for the streak and, next, chores and allowance. And because grandparents span households, each family seeds the next one."
