# Kin: recording script (about 2:45)

Record yourself on the laptop camera, face centered, in a quiet spot. Read it like you're talking to one friend, not reading.
Your video goes in a circle in the bottom-left corner; the product plays behind you, section by section.
**Keep to the sections in order.** Each one has a matching product shot, so if a section runs a little long or short, the video adapts.

---

### 1 · Hook (≈12s) · *screen: the Kin welcome screen*
> Every family has a human router. Usually it's Mom. She notices the eggs are gone, remembers Jake's peanut allergy, knows Dad drives past Trader Joe's, and then texts, reminds and re-reminds, every single day.

### 2 · What Kin is (≈18s) · *screen: Home ("2 things on you", the Kin brief, Your home)*
> This is Kin, a household AI. One private brain for the whole family. Everything each of us knows goes into one memory that's shared only inside the household. People open ChatGPT every day to think. Families will open Kin every day to know what's going on at home: what's on me today, what's on everyone else, and what the house needs.

### 3 · Tell it once (≈25s) · *screen: two phones, Sarah types, Mike's phone buzzes*
> Watch. Sarah types one line: "We're out of eggs, and Jake needs a snack for tomorrow's field trip." Kin remembers Mike passes Trader Joe's at six and that Jake is allergic to peanuts. So it puts eggs and a **nut-free** snack on Mike, and his phone gets a live nudge, instantly. When Mike checks it off, Sarah sees "Mike finished." Nobody had to call or text anyone.

### 4 · Snap it (≈14s) · *screen: a school flyer turns into tasks*
> Kin also sees. Jake brings home a field-trip flyer; we snap it. Kin turns it into the event, the permission slip, the fifteen dollars and a nut-free lunch, each assigned to the right person.

### 5 · The house talks too (≈15s) · *screen: "Your home" devices, the robot vacuum event*
> And it's not just people. The fridge, the door camera, the robot vacuum and the TV report to Kin through one webhook. The vacuum says it's stuck under Jake's bed; Kin tells Jake. Any smart-home hub can plug in.

### 6 · Parents decide (≈15s) · *screen: House rules in the Family Brain, Kin telling Jake "homework first"*
> Parents run the house, so Kin takes their side. Parents set house rules, like homework before games. When Jake asks to play Fortnite, Kin says not yet. If someone ignores a chore, Kin follows up on its own, and then tells a parent.

### 7 · Private stays private (≈12s) · *screen: Mike's "Just me" chat*
> And families keep secrets. Mike plans Sarah's surprise fortieth in "Just me". Kin remembers it and reminds him, but it never shows up on Sarah's phone.

### 8 · How we built it with the sponsors (≈40s) · *screen: architecture diagram, Capy PR, Revyl cloud iPhone*
> We built this today with the sponsor stack.
> **Capy** is an AI software engineer: you give it a task and it opens a pull request. Capy wrote our webhook-security and memory-privacy PR, which we reviewed and merged.
> **Revyl** is a mobile dev platform: it builds the iOS app in the cloud, runs it on a real cloud iPhone and tests it in plain English. Revyl caught a real iOS bug, an invisible toggle, that we fixed and re-verified.
> **Convex** is the reactive backend: every list, chat and nudge is a live query, so every family phone updates instantly. It also runs the AI, the autopilot follow-ups and the device webhook.
> **Supermemory** is the memory engine: one namespace for the family and a private one for each person, so Kin's memory gets better every day.
> And **Whop** runs payments: Kin Plus is $4.99 a month per family.

### 9 · Why every day + close (≈14s) · *screen: the "Scan to try Kin" QR*
> Things land on a family every single day, and Kin is where they go. Google Photos remembered your life; Kin remembers your family's. Scan the code and join the Miller family live. Thank you.
